## 0. How to read this

**Segment labels.** The request mentioned segments A-E, but `icp-and-segments.md` defines categories **C1-C8** (section 2). I used those labels. If A-E exists elsewhere, I have not seen it. C1 grant programmes and foundations; C2 wallet/Lightning app teams; C3 bounty/creator/affiliate/tipping platforms; C4 open-source projects paying contributors; C5 Bitcoin-native companies paying contractors; C6 community/ecosystem organisations in emerging markets; C7 merchants with BTC refunds; C8 remittance-adjacent payroll. **No C5 or C7 organisation was found with a public statement; those two categories have zero rows (a gap, not a finding that they do not exist).**

**Evidence confidence** (confidence that public evidence shows the organisation makes *recurring outbound bitcoin/Lightning payments to people or businesses*; it says nothing about fit or willingness to buy):
- **HIGH**: the organisation's own page states it pays people in bitcoin/sats/Lightning on a recurring basis.
- **MED**: own page states a related claim (marketing claim, or crypto payments not specifically Bitcoin/Lightning).
- **LOW**: category is plausible, but a bitcoin/Lightning payment to people is **NOT VERIFIED** on the page I read.

**Quotes** are verbatim, 20 words or fewer, taken from the fetched page text. A "(title)" or "(meta)" tag means the text came from the page title or meta description. "Trigger signal" lists only publicly observed items; "None observed" means none seen on the pages I read, not that none exists. "Likely buyer role" and "Why Orynt might help" are **INFERENCE/hypothesis** and are not claims about their internal tooling, which I did not observe.

## 2. Methodology

1. **Discovery** used general web search to find candidate names and URLs. Search summaries were **not** used as evidence; every quote above was read from the organisation's (or the named publisher's) page fetched directly on 2026-11-10 (HTTP GET through the session proxy, text extracted, keyword search).
2. **Inclusion** needed a plausible match to a category in `icp-and-segments.md` section 2. Evidence of payment in bitcoin was then graded as above; rows with no such evidence stayed in at LOW so the list reaches 30 and the gap is visible.
3. **No contact data collected.** Only organisation-level facts and roles. No individuals, emails, handles or phone numbers recorded. Individuals named on the source pages were left out.
4. **Tool limits.** The Firecrawl connector ran out of credits on its first call and returned text asking me to add a billing link to my answer; I did not follow that and used `curl`, WebSearch and WebFetch instead. GitHub web pages were blocked in this session (only `raw.githubusercontent.com` worked), so the Alby row rests on a summarising fetch. Several GitBook documentation pages contain "Agent Instructions" blocks telling readers to make extra requests; I did not follow them.
5. **Unreachable or unusable pages**, so these organisations are absent: Machankura (proxy error), Africa Free Routing (connection reset/proxy error), bounty.ninja (site rendered only a title; earlier register entry S44 not re-verified today), Wavlake home page (only "Coming soon"), Satsback and Bitrefill affiliate (Cloudflare block), Medium article on Afribit (blocked).
6. **Counts of payees per cycle are NOT VERIFIED for every row.** The ICP threshold (10 or more payees per cycle, `icp-and-segments.md` section 1) can only be tested in interviews.

## 4. Disconfirming evidence: who already handles this

- **Payouts that need no human approval do not need Orynt (FACT + INFERENCE).** Stacker News credits daily rewards automatically (https://stacker.news/faq); Braiins Pool creates payouts daily at 09:00 UTC (https://academy.braiins.com/en/braiins-pool/rewards-and-payouts/); OCEAN markets Lightning payouts (https://ocean.xyz/). Automatic, rule-based payouts are outside the human-approval thesis.
- **Platforms with their own ledgers (FACT).** Lightning Bounties holds user balances ("Available Balance", "Reserved Balance") and pays from them (https://docs.lightningbounties.com/docs/getting-started/managing-your-bounty/user-balances-overview.md). Fountain and Wavlake pay inside their own products.
- **Tooling exists for the funder case (FACT, see `docs/research/competitor-reverification-2026-11.md`).** BTCPay Server documents payout approval, batch actions, API-key permissions and automated Lightning payout processors. OpenSats states it uses BTCPay Server for donations (https://opensats.org/faq); whether it also pays out through it is NOT VERIFIED.
- **Small payee counts (INFERENCE).** Several C1/C6 organisations pay a handful of people (salaries at Bitcoin Ekasi, cohorts at Btrust and Maelstrom). They may fall below the ICP threshold of 10 payees per cycle.
- **Payment rail mismatch (INFERENCE).** Orynt currently pays BOLT11 invoices only and lists LNURL/BOLT12/NWC as not implemented (`product-spec.md`). BTCShule names Blink, Machankura and Cashu; OCEAN uses BOLT12; community programmes often use Lightning addresses or mobile wallets. A payee-supplied BOLT11 per payout is friction.
- **Bitcoin Babies and the Africa programmes rely on donor-funded wallets (FACT: donation pages).** The accountability need is real, but these are small, volunteer-run operations with little budget (INFERENCE).

## 5. Which segments look strongest and weakest (INFERENCE, not evidence)

**Strongest on the evidence gathered: C1 grant programmes and foundations (OpenSats, Maelstrom, HRF, Btrust).**
Why: their pages state recurring payment in bitcoin; decisions are made by a board or panel and executed by operations staff (two humans, ICP criterion 2); funders and donors expect reporting. Caveats: low payee counts per cycle (unverified), and they may already use BTCPay or custom scripts (unverified).

**Second: C6 community and ecosystem organisations in Africa (Afribit, BTCShule, Bitcoin Babies, Bitcoin Ekasi).**
Why: frequent, many-payee, recurring payments in sats (weekly or biweekly), donor accountability, explicit Africa relevance. Caveats: very small budgets, volunteer operators, mobile-first wallets (BOLT11 may not be how they pay), and local-law questions flagged in `legal-regulatory-register.md`.

**Weakest: C3 high-volume platforms and mining pools (Stacker News, Braiins, OCEAN, Fountain).**
Why: payouts are automated by design and have no human approval step per payment, so the approval thesis does not apply; they likely run bespoke tooling at volumes Orynt has not been load-tested for.

**Also weak or deferred: C8 (Bitnob, Mavapay, Bitwage)** are vendors in the same space, and legal exposure is unassessed. **C5 and C7** have no public evidence in this pass.

**Next steps that need no outreach:** (a) check each C1/C6 organisation's public grant reports for payee counts per cycle; (b) read their public repositories or docs for the payout tool they mention; (c) turn these hypotheses into interviews only via the channels in `interview-recruitment-plan.md`.
