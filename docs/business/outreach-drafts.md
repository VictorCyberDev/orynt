## 1. Rules for use

1. **One message per organisation. Ever.** No follow-up, no reminder, no second channel (do not email *and* use the form).
2. **Stop on any decline or any signal of "no".** Silence is also "no". Record it; do not re-contact in this validation window.
3. **No tracking.** Plain text only. No hyperlinks, no images, no attachments, no read-receipts, no tracked links, no pixels, no scheduling-tool links. Offer to agree a time by reply. The drafts name the page they cite as plain text (for example "opensats.org/newsletter/2026-Q2") so the reader can check it; send in plain-text mode so mail clients do not turn it into a tracked link, or delete the path if in doubt.
4. **Never ask for** wallet keys, seeds, macaroons, API tokens, invoices, payee names or payee data. The messages say so.
5. **Never imply** a relationship, endorsement, customers, partners, funding or an existing pilot.
6. **No incentive** is offered. If the owner decides to offer one, state it in the message and make it independent of what is said.
7. **Vulnerable payees.** Bitcoin Babies and BTC Shule pay mothers, children and households. Do not ask who they are, how many by name, or where; ask only about the *process*.
8. **Competitor and conflict check** before sending: if the organisation is also a vendor in this space, say so in your notes and exclude it from threshold counts.
9. **Log it** in the channel log and pipeline tracker in `interview-recruitment-plan.md` section 8 using participant codes, and update `validation-scorecard.md` counters only after a message is actually sent.

## 2. Compliance note (not legal advice)

- **Who you are.** Real sender name, a working reply address that you own, and a postal address in the signature. CAN-SPAM-style rules ask for honest headers, a non-deceptive subject line, identification of the sender, a valid postal address and a working opt-out honoured promptly (US law covers *commercial* messages; this is research, but following the same hygiene costs nothing). GDPR (for EU/UK recipients) and the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, Kenya's Data Protection Act 2019 and South Africa's POPIA (for the recipients in those countries) should be treated as applying to any personal data in replies. Which ones apply to which recipient is a question for counsel (`legal-regulatory-register.md`, items NG4 and EU2); nothing here has been reviewed by counsel.
- **Honest subject.** The subject must say it is a research request, not a sales message.
- **Minimal data.** The only personal data created is the recipient's reply and, if they agree, the interview notes. Store contact details separately from notes, use participant codes in the repository, and follow the retention and deletion terms in `interview-guide.md` section 3 (those figures are proposals pending counsel).
- **Opt-out.** Every message includes a one-line opt-out. Honour it at once, delete the contact record on request and confirm.
- **Role mailboxes.** The addresses below are organisation-published mailboxes, not personal addresses. Do not guess other addresses, do not look up staff on social networks to forward the message, and do not copy anyone.
- **Third parties.** Never forward a reply, and never name an organisation as a participant without separate written permission.

## 3. Common structure used by every draft

1. Who I am, in one sentence, with the early-stage disclosure.
2. Why this organisation (one public fact, with where it was read).
3. What I am asking (20 minutes; three topics: the last uncertain or failed payment, how you reconcile, how you approve).
4. What I am not claiming and not asking for.
5. Easy opt-out. No further message.
